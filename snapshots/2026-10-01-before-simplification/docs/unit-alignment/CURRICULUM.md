## Binding corridor and the legitimate claim for this activity

The 2027 writing corridor includes two fields: the individual and society (identity and diversity), and UK politics/culture/society between tradition and change. The UK historical sequence covers the Elizabethan Age, Empire and postcolonial Britain; its current strand concerns identity and political, cultural and social developments. The gN target is B2. Writing still spans all AFB, with emphasis on AFB II. The actual exam also assesses German-to-English mediation and speaking. Its overall weights are 55/25/20; within writing, language/content are 60/40. These are exam requirements, not a required weighting for this learning activity. [2027 rules, pp. 1–3](https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/mm/downloads/III-Bildungsministerium/Zentrale_Abschluesse/2027_Pruefungsregelungen_Abitur_allgbild_Schulen_neu.zip).

Recommended scope statement:

> A 45-minute Q2 English investigation at grundlegendes Niveau. Students read selected evidence about the British Empire and postcolonial Britain, explain historical connections, and write a short, supported judgement about present-day British identities. It contributes to the UK topic corridor for Abitur 2027.

The six stops supply an intelligible bridge from imperial institutions to postcolonial citizenship and memory. They do **not** demonstrate full coverage of the Elizabethan Age, UK government, devolution, Brexit, contemporary social and cultural developments, the other IQB field, or the literature strand. Elizabeth I's 1600 Company charter is a useful connection to expansion, but naming its monarch is not substantial Elizabethan study. Location within Q2 and the sequence of lessons are school/teacher planning decisions, not a state-prescribed six-stop order.

## Operators and AFB: implementation guidance

Use the **2026 examination-specific operator list**, not only the older 2014 appendix. Its writing definitions distinguish concise retrieval, evidence-based explanation/analysis and supported judgement. _Analyse/examine_ relates content to relevant language/form; _discuss_ weighs both sides; _evaluate_ requires criteria. [2027 rules, pp. 4–5](https://www.schleswig-holstein.de/mm/downloads/III-Bildungsministerium/Zentrale_Abschluesse/2027_Pruefungsregelungen_Abitur_allgbild_Schulen_neu.zip).

The following assignments are **recommendations for this short activity**, not ministry-mandated prompts:

| Current stop             | Best fit and concrete adjustment                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Trade becomes rule       | **Explain** one supported connection between revenue and power. Use **outline** only for a genuinely concise preliminary factual account. Avoid labelling it language analysis.                                                                                                                               |
| Freedom and memory       | **Explain** how the museum sentence assigns agency; **comment on** the adequacy of that account using resistance plus abolition/compensation evidence. Ask explicitly what the verb “gave” makes the reader notice or overlook.                                                                               |
| India / Canada           | **Explain** the difference concealed by one map colour. For a genuine small analysis element, ask how “equal and impartial” presents royal rule and why this rhetorical claim is not proof of implementation. Do not require a full textual comparison when the Canada item is only a constitutional summary. |
| Independence / partition | **Explain** the difference between constitutional change and lived experience, using one source detail. Keep the present-day connection tentative when the source does not establish it.                                                                                                                      |
| Windrush / belonging     | **Explain** the relation of legal status and experienced belonging using the person's record or review. Preserve individual experience; avoid reducing a diverse group to one representative identity.                                                                                                        |
| Kenya / memory           | **Explain** what a dated official statement can establish. One source limitation is enough for the brief answer; an additional Hong Kong comparison should not displace the central reasoning.                                                                                                                |
| Final synthesis          | **Assess** the extent to which empire helps explain present-day British identities; use evidence and a relevant complication. **Comment on** the quoted claim is another valid teacher choice. Use **discuss** only if the prompt genuinely requires consideration of both sides.                             |

A clear AFB progression can be achieved within the existing route: retrieve one relevant detail; explain its significance and the source's limits; reach a supported judgement. gN does not mean eliminating explanation or judgement, and AFB labels are not automatic quality scores.

### What the newly read research report changes or clarifies

1. **Every overseas case needs a UK-facing purpose.** India, Jamaica, Canada, Kenya or Hong Kong are useful evidence for empire, but the activity’s synthesis must return to British identities, institutions, migration or public memory. Do not let the atlas become a disconnected global history quiz. The connection may be a carefully labelled hypothesis when the available source cannot establish present attitudes. This follows the planning rationale on research-report **PDF p. 3**, not a requirement that all historians adopt one interpretation.
2. **The report’s coverage headings are a planning checklist, not additional state legislation.** Its “sensible minimum coverage” on **PDF p. 4** includes topics such as civilising mission, Commonwealth and institutional racism. The actual state corridor is broader and less prescriptive. A single 45-minute component does not need a separate task for every item on that checklist. The report’s **6–10 Elizabethan lessons** recommendation on **PDF p. 3** also differs from the detailed plans’ **2–3 hours** on **PDF p. 3**; both are pedagogical planning alternatives, not official quotas.
3. **No named compulsory reading is not permission to omit literature from the course.** The research report **PDF p. 3** correctly finds no fixed novel, drama or text list in the cited UK corridor. The wider subject curriculum still includes literature and the arts. This mostly documentary route can be one component; it should not claim to satisfy the course’s literary work by linking to an optional extract.
4. **No purchasing or license dependency is necessary.** The report’s publisher comparisons and procurement scenarios (**PDF pp. 19–21**) describe choices for a complete course. They do not require this activity to reproduce, purchase or integrate those commercial texts. Retain accessible core source cards; named classroom readings can be optional references to the class copies.
5. **Do not import the report’s semester scale.** Its illustrative **14–16-week** sequence (**PDF pp. 20–21**), additional media and varied assessment products are useful surrounding context. They do not alter the authorized 45-minute route. A 45-minute rallye may occupy part of a 60-minute lesson, with orientation and debrief explicitly outside its timing.
6. **Current rules settle examination claims.** The research report’s reference **S01** points to the official supporting handout, whereas the latest independently verified examination specification is the **1 August 2026** gN document in the ministry’s September ZIP. Use that specification for operator wording and examination assessment. Product recommendations, prices, availability and dated current-politics descriptions in the August research report are not facts to import into the app without separate verification; none is needed to complete this focused route.

The supplementary **`Q2_Englisch_UK_5_Verlaufsplanungen_2026_27.pdf`**, PDF pp. 2–3, corroborates the detailed plans’ architecture, but it is no longer the principal plan evidence. The optional vocabulary/classroom texts may support instruction without adding required reading to the core timing.

## Priority recommendations for the application and teacher guide

1. **Name the educational fit precisely.** Add Q2 English, SH, gN/B2 and the UK corridor to teacher guidance. Say “contributes to” or “practises selected aspects”; avoid “covers the Q2 curriculum” or “complete Abitur preparation”. Use the August 2026 rules as the dated reference.
2. **Protect the 12-minute final argument.** The existing 33 + 12 minute model already consumes the complete activity. Students currently produce roughly 410–590 target words while navigating, reading, selecting sources and thinking. Treat that as an ambitious classroom estimate, not a verified completion guarantee. Reduce each station to one main reasoning move; target the indicated passage, and reserve independent web searching and extended comparisons for follow-up. Do not silently add speaking, mediation, an additional essay or full classroom texts.
3. **Make the language work observable.** Include short, optional sentence frames such as “The source shows …, but cannot establish …”, “This suggests … rather than proving …”, and “Although …, …”. Use the supplementary user vocabulary sheet as an optional class reference. Add a compact check for clear connections, precise terms and qualification rather than a second long written product.
4. **Strengthen one authentic micro-analysis.** Use the existing brief proclamation quotation or the museum sentence to ask about language and perspective. Clearly distinguish the invented museum teaching prompt, original quotation and editorial summary. Never ask students to analyse rhetorical devices supposedly present in a summary as if they were the original document.
5. **Keep assessment claims honest.** The current 40-point rubric can record historical reasoning and source use formatively. It is not the official Abitur marking scheme or a B2 certification. If the teacher turns the work into a marked English writing assessment, language needs separately specified criteria and a teacher decision on appropriate assessment conditions; merely imposing the exam's 60:40 ratio on tiny station answers would not create an exam.
6. **Make source limits help the argument.** Require source naming in the answer, not only clicking “save”. Accept a specific limitation linked to the pupil’s inference; reject empty “biased” labels. Keep the current safeguards against treating a state statement as an opinion poll and against requiring one political conclusion.
7. **Place additions outside the core route.** Teacher follow-ups may include a paired monologue/dialogue, a genuine German-to-English mediation task, or a separately sourced contemporary case. Each must have its own material, instructions and time budget before its competence can be claimed. Preserve the existing recoverable draft and hand-in workflow when prompts change.

**Acceptance boundary:** a sound result is an accessible, source-aware English learning activity that connects selected Empire cases to postcolonial identity and memory. It remains a component of the larger unit, with explicit limits on historical breadth, media/genre coverage, assessed language and examination readiness.
