## Finding

The existing rallye is already closely aligned with the handbook's historical and interpretative frame. It connects empire to present institutions and belonging, recognises colonised people's agency, distinguishes official claims from people's experiences, and explicitly rejects a single British state of mind. These features should survive revision. The useful changes are making **continuity, transformation and contestation** explicit; adding a small amount of close language analysis; and locating the activity within the broader English unit. Adding many more historical cases would work against the handbook's own preference for selective, transferable knowledge and against the 45-minute contract.

## Prioritised changes within the existing 45 minutes

1. **Make the conceptual route visible.** Suggested concise framing: “Track what continues, what changes, and who challenges the story. Postcolonial Britain means Britain shaped by continuing and changing relationships created through empire.” This is a proposed paraphrase of the teaching frame, not a handbook quotation.
2. **Make one or two tasks unmistakably English analysis.** Keep Stop 2's constructed panel but ask what “gave” does to agency; use Stop 3's existing quotation to examine “all” and the difference between promise and practice. Connect language, historical context and interpretative effect. Do not reduce the work to naming a device.
3. **Keep plural identities in the marking, not only the preface.** The current final rubric already does this well. Credit “this government statement”, “this author”, “some families whose testimony…” and explained differences within groups; do not make unsupported statements about “the British”, “migrants” or “the Scots” a route to full marks.
4. **Protect agency alongside injury.** Retain resistance, negotiation, family choices and cultural creation. Avoid turning every case into passive suffering followed by British recognition. Keep the teacher's point about Kenyan survivors pursuing redress.
5. **Use the final answer as a bridge to the unit's text work.** Name the primary plans' appropriate preceding or following reading in teacher guidance once the lesson-plan owner has fixed placement. Supplementary Britishness texts may illustrate transfer, but must not define the primary task. The final answer can complicate a representation without importing the text or adding a new compulsory reading inside the timed route.
6. **Keep alternative explanations genuine.** Class, region, religion, generation, domestic institutions and later policy decisions can qualify an argument. Students must explain relevance, not merely list these words. Preserve the current acceptance of evidence-based disagreement.

## Specific consistency fix for the teacher/source owners

At initial review the teacher key and student card identified different reproductions of Victoria's proclamation. `teacher-content.js` (source declarations near lines 26–33 and the Stop 3 note near line 133) explained an 1891 British Columbia petition and one British Library catalogue record. `rallye-resources.js` (the `rule-proclamation` card, near lines 84–101) supplied a Tamil Digital Library scan, Appendix E, and another catalogue record. Both can potentially be legitimate sources, but the teacher note described neither the displayed document nor the student's actual reading path. Make the teacher note match the displayed edition and keep alternative reproductions explicitly labelled as alternatives. This is a coherence finding, not a judgement on either website's authenticity; live source verification belongs to the source reviewer. The issue has been sent directly to both `unit_teacher` and `unit_sources`; this report does not assert the later implementation status.

## Source-use limits

The handbook is a teacher preparation document (p. 2), not a student reading or replacement lesson sequence. It makes date-sensitive office-holder and policy assertions on pp. 2–3, 103 and 110; this review does not verify or recommend importing them. Its nationality and Windrush summaries on pp. 48, 50 and 118 should not displace the current application's more precise arrival/assent/effective-date distinction. Use direct historical sources for that legal chronology.

The current task does not need new compulsory units on Elizabethan theatre, devolution, monarchy, Brexit, regional languages, contemporary gender law or all postcolonial theory. The handbook explicitly prioritises selection (pp. 13, 102, 117). The relevant success criterion is a well-supported historical bridge and a qualified English argument, not the number of handbook chapters represented.
