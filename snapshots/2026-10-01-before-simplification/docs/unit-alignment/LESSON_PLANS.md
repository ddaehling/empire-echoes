## What the plans actually commit to

Both documents describe **17 weeks of two separate 60-minute lessons**, with 28 core, two flex and four reserve lessons. The self-study slots S3/S4 count as lessons. The examination is a separate block after S13 in A/B/D or after S14 in C/E; it is not a normal 60-minute lesson and may consume available course time. C1-2; D1-2. The recommendation of B in C2/D2 is advice within the document, not evidence that B was adopted.

The shared planning question is how historical knowledge explains contemporary UK identities, institutions and debates. The approximate content weights in C3/D3 are Elizabethan Age 2-3 lessons, Empire 3-4, postcolonial Britain 6-8, political developments 5-7, cultural/social developments 5-7, and synthesis/examination transfer 2-3. These are overlapping planning strands, not quantities to add into a new compulsory total. An Empire activity should not claim that it alone covers the 28-lesson core.

The plans specify B2/gN as their intended level. Their common principles are a clear question followed by a primary competence and then a suitable material; repeated short speaking cycles; purposeful products; interleaving of reading, listening, viewing, speaking, writing and mediation; and evidence of the learning process. They explicitly say that long texts are shortened or prepared as home reading, and that a film or listening lesson is not interchangeable with a reading lesson. C2-3; D2-3, 29-30.

The individual Empire sheets formulate three connected aims:

1. Explain causes, ideologies, violence, exploitation, resistance, decolonisation and contested memory, without portraying Empire as one uniform project.
2. Use precise evidence to build a differentiated answer; distinguish time, place, actors, forms of rule and limits of evidence.
3. Make an uncertainty, qualification or counterposition visible. In speaking lessons, respond to questions and revise an answer after feedback; in viewing lessons, distinguish observation from interpretation and explain choices in the visual medium.

These aims appear, with competency-specific wording, in A S9/D40, B S21/D87, C S24/D125, D S17/D153 and E S8/D179. The expected vocabulary includes _imperialism, colonial rule, extraction, civilising mission, resistance,_ and _decolonisation_. The sheets caution against a simple pride-versus-shame vote. They ask who benefited, who bore costs, and whose evidence is available.

## Concrete timing adaptation

The 45-minute student activity remains **33 minutes across the six stops plus 12 minutes for the final argument**. Do not silently make the final argument homework or an optional extra. For a normal 60-minute lesson use this proposed wrapper:

| Minutes | Action and purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0-5     | Retrieve one claim from the preceding lesson, clarify UK/GB/England where needed, and demonstrate the source notebook once. State that the atlas is evidence about political relationships and has limits.                                            |
| 5-38    | Six rallye stops at their existing 5/6/6/6/5/5-minute allocations. Use the short core evidence already available in the app; an original source is inspected with a focused question, not read in full.                                               |
| 38-50   | Required final argument: two minutes to select evidence, eight to write, two to check.                                                                                                                                                                |
| 50-58   | Simultaneous pair oral defences: each learner gives a 60-second claim with named evidence, receives a question, responds, then roles switch. Use remaining time for one revised sentence. The teacher samples pairs; this is not a whole-class queue. |
| 58-60   | Exit: one revised claim or causal connection, one source limitation, and one question for the next named lesson. Save/export according to the course's submission routine.                                                                            |

This is a **documented adaptation of one lesson**, not the original minute sequence. A S9 and E S8 originally allocate much more time to dialogue; B S21, C S24 and D S17 originally use two viewing passes, reconstruction and visual analysis. The rallye emphasises reading/media evaluation and writing, with an oral component in the wrapper. Therefore the original competency balance totals cannot simply be copied unchanged after replacement. If the original viewing or speaking objective must be preserved in full, retain that lesson and place the rallye in a genuinely available flex/revision slot; do not claim both full lessons fit in 60 minutes.

The short core evidence and a practised interface are essential to the 45-minute intention. The current content asks for 45-65 words per stop and 140-200 words at the end: roughly 410-590 target words, plus reading, maps and notebook use. This is plausible only as a tightly bounded short-response activity for prepared B2 learners, not as open-ended web research. The timings are planning targets, not evidence from a classroom trial. Full original readings, extensive source searches and a whole-class discussion need separate time. Use optional extension research after completion, not as a hidden precondition.

For a school with an actual 45-minute bell, the activity occupies the entire slot; setup must already be familiar and the oral defence can open the next planned lesson. Do not squeeze the extra 15-minute wrapper into those 45 minutes. Do not shorten the final argument to conceal setup overruns.

## Self-study commitments are separate

The common week-2 design is explicit in C4 and D4:

- **S2 preparation:** assign and document a different numbered source and individual question for each learner.
- **S3:** handwritten four-colour annotation distinguishing claim, evidence, context and uncertainty; three original questions; evidence log; submit a photograph and bring the original in S5. The table budgets 10 minutes for checking the task, 25 for annotation, 15 for evidence log and 10 for upload/questions.
- **S4:** three self-documented media/everyday representations, without photographing people; a one-take 90-second audio and a source/process log. The tables budget 10+15+15+15+5 minutes for collecting, source protocol, recording, process log and submission check.
- **S5:** jigsaw plus a spontaneous 60-second defence and device-free synthesis. The quality of visible thinking is central, not polish of a home product.

Individual variants are A D34-36, B D69-71, C D104-106, D D139-141 and E D174-176. In C the topic is specifically regional/devolution evidence; in D it is cultural evidence. These cannot all be relabelled an Empire webquest without altering the plans.

The app's notebook, drafts and export can contribute process evidence for this later activity, but saved text and completed boxes do not fulfil the distinct handwriting, individually allocated source, self-collected representation, audio and spontaneous verification requirements. The rallye should not replace S3 and S4 or be described as the whole KI-robust self-study phase. If used for homework instead, retain the oral verification and be explicit that this is a changed placement.

## Additional findings from the primary textbook research report

It distinguishes a curated gN selection with clear scaffolding from more extensive eN primary sources and theory. The rallye should therefore supply manageable evidence for a question about identity, not accumulate all the report's possible materials.

### Progression and timing distinction

R20-21 proposes a broad sequence: Britishness and nations; Elizabethan power/worldview/theatre; expansion and Empire; contested memory; migration and belonging; regional/Union issues; Brexit; tradition under pressure; synthesis. The rallye fits the expansion-to-memory transition or later historical retrieval. Its final answer makes a first connection to belonging that subsequent materials can complicate.

There is a concrete planning inconsistency to avoid importing: R20 labels the example **14-16 weeks**, but the separate durations on R20-21 add to **15-18 weeks** if treated as sequential phases. Also, R3's suggested 6-10 additional Elizabethan teaching hours for an Abi-Box purchase is a broad procurement scenario, whereas D3 gives a compact 2-3-lesson guide within its specific 28-core-lesson model. These describe different planning scales. Use the explicit D lesson slots for the 60-minute integration above; do not add every broad research-report duration to them or claim the report alone fixes a particular course calendar.

## App alignment: retain, add in teacher guidance, avoid claiming

The current content already supports the plans' causal and critical direction: Company taxation and coercion; resistance and abolition; unequal imperial governance; independence and displacement; Windrush and belonging; and continuing responsibility/memory. Its final answer requires a judgement, three stops, two past-to-present connections, a complication and a source limitation. These align well with a historical bridge into plural British identities.

The material also respects the plans' warning against a simple national pride/shame verdict: the final rubric rewards supported disagreement and plural identities. The teacher should assess historical mechanism and source use, not demand one political conclusion. The dated official Kenya statement, for example, supports an official position at a particular time, not a claim about everybody's contemporary views.

The remaining alignment work belongs mainly in task framing and teacher support:

1. Name the alternative lesson locations above and keep them alternatives. Do not call the rallye “the Q2 unit” or automatically “lesson 9.”
2. Show the 45-minute allocation including the final, and the optional 60-minute wrapper. Describe the oral defence as a concrete course connection.
3. Provide a brief retrieve-and-return link to the teacher's existing Britishness/identity materials and the next specific textbook lesson. Students need to distinguish stereotype, representation and an evidenced historical explanation.
4. Keep source provenance and a field for what a source cannot establish. This directly matches D206's T1 evidence log and D207's T6 source reliability ladder.
5. Keep the historical argument as formative course work. The final task offers evidence-based judgement practice, but does not reproduce the planned unseen-text analysis examination, listening assessment, literary analysis or full speaking test. Examination models and their conditions are in C25/D25.
