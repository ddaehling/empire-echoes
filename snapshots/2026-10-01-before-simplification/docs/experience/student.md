# Twenty minutes, cold, as a fifteen-year-old

**Who I am for this document.** Year 11. I know Britain had an empire, I know India and
Australia were in it, and that's about the lot. A teacher sent me a link and said "look at
this for homework." Nobody told me what to do when I got here. I am on a school laptop:
**1366 × 768**, Chrome, no headphones, and I would rather be doing something else.

**How this was produced.** Real driving of the real app at `http://localhost:8777/app/` via
`tools/inspect.js`, cold context every time (empty localStorage, no prior state), reading
every PNG. Viewports tested: 1366×768, 1920×1080, 390×844. Every measurement below is a
number the browser gave me, not an impression.

---

## Part 1 — the first 90 seconds, second by second

**0:00** The page is already finished loading. There is no animation, no title card, no
"start here", no name of a lesson. It is just *there*, complete, all of it at once.

My eye goes — honestly — nowhere. It bounces. I count later, deliberately: there are
**81 interactive controls inside the viewport**, **666 words of text**, **165 separate text
nodes** and **nine different font sizes** (8px to 27px) on screen at second zero. Nothing is
bigger or louder than anything else except the word **1900**, which is in the bottom-left
corner where I am not looking.

**0:03** I find the map because it's the only picture. It is small. Later I measure it:
the drawn plate is **704 × 297 px** inside a 946 × 361 stage. That's **8% of my screen's
area.** The thing the whole app is named after is smaller than a YouTube thumbnail row.
Africa is about 90px tall. I cannot read most of the labels without leaning in.

**0:08** Top-left, in a box: **ASK THESE THREE OF ANY IMPERIAL MAP**. Three numbered
questions in italics with the answers already printed next to them. I do not know what an
"imperial map" is yet. I have not been shown one. This is a critical-thinking exercise
about a thing I have not looked at for five seconds. I skip it. *(This is a shame, because
it is one of the best ideas in the app. It is just three minutes too early.)*

**0:14** Below it, another box: **How to read this map · claimed — degree 1+, not informal ·
FOLD**. Then `182 of 302 units · 132 territories · 25 million km² ≈ 109× Great Britain ·
people — no figure`. I do not know what a "unit" is. I do not know why it says
"people — no figure" — is that a bug? Is that a fact? "109× Great Britain" is the only
number here that means anything to me and it is the fourth item in the line.

**0:22** Top-right, a third box: **"British"** with four buttons — 1 claimed / 2
administered / 3 controlled / 4 influenced — and a sentence under it that is **cut off
mid-word**: *"Everywhere Britain said was hers — from a governor with an army to a resident
with a…"*. I don't press it, because I don't know what pressing it would do and the
explanation of what it would do is the thing that's truncated.

**0:30** I read the bottom-left. **1900.** So this is 1900. Nobody told me that was going
to be the starting point or why. My assumption is the map shows the whole empire ever. It
does not. (It shows 1900. I only work this out at about minute six.)

**0:35** Four cards in a row across the middle: Transvaal Colony, Orange River Colony,
Tonga, Niue. All four have the same shape and the same red heading and all four end in "…"
and then **the rest of this account →**. Four identical promises. Above them: `12 acts
dated 1900 · 6 change the m… · +4 units · 3 records dated here, no map change · 2 further
records of these same ac…` — two of the five clauses in that line are themselves truncated.

**0:48** The bottom third of the screen is a wall of tiny marks. Two rows of ticks, some
gold circles with 2 3 4 5 6 in **8px type**, a rail of red and blue hairs, and four
overlapping labelled bars (I The Atlantic empire / II The Company empire / III The imperial
empire / IV Dissolution). I later measure this: **the time control alone is 321 of my 768
pixels — 42% of my screen.** I have no idea which of these five horizontal things I am
supposed to touch.

**0:55** I notice the whole time area is denser and busier than the map. That feels wrong
and I can't say why.

**1:05** I scroll. The page moves **23 pixels** and stops. `scrollHeight` is 791 against
a 768 viewport. There is no more app. Everything there is, is what's here. A sentence at the
very bottom — *"One engine: the imperial empire. Industrial output needing markets, steam
and telegraph shrinking distance…"* — is **half cut off by the bottom edge of the window**
and cannot be fully revealed. That sentence turns out to be one of the four best sentences
in the app.

**1:20** I click on the biggest red thing I can see, which is in India, because India is the
one I've heard of.

**1:28** A panel slides in from the right and **three things break at once**:
- the whole left column re-lays out and the COLOURS legend is **sliced in half horizontally**
  — the top of the swatches is visible and the bottom is cut off by the edge of the panel;
- the "British" 1/2/3/4 switch **jumps out of the right rail and lands in the middle of the
  screen**, on top of where the map bottom used to be, with its explanation still truncated;
- the map controls (P S W H E, + − ◻) end up in a floating white box overlapping the map's
  bottom-right corner;
- and the four change-cards go from four to two.

**1:30** And the panel says: **"Goa under British garrison."** Underneath, in italics:
***"Britain held nothing here in 1900."***

I clicked on the middle of British India, in the year 1900, and got a place that was not
British in 1900. This is my first interaction with the app and its answer is "not this."

## Part 2 — minute by minute, to twenty

**Minute 2.** I read the Goa panel anyway because it's there. It's good, actually — 1799,
occupied to keep the French out, handed back in 1813, Portugal kept it until 1961. I did not
know that. But it is not what I asked for and it is not on the story I am here for. The
panel says **"14 sections ↓"** in the corner. Fourteen. I measure it later: this panel is
**5,840 pixels of scroll inside a 712-pixel window — 8.2 screenfuls for one place**.

**Minute 3.** I scroll it. About a third of the way down there's something genuinely
brilliant: **"4 TEXTS WRITTEN AT THE TIME"**, then a document — an administrative despatch
from Warren Hastings to the shareholders' board — and then **"What do you think this one was
made for?"** with three buttons. That is the single best thing I meet all session. It is
about two thousand pixels down a side-column on a screen where the other 70% is still
showing a map I've stopped looking at.

**Minute 4.** I close it. I press **Play**, because Play is a thing I understand.

**Minute 5.** Thirty seconds of Play gets me from **1900 to 1912**. That's 2.5 seconds a
year. At this rate the whole story, 1600 to 1997, is a **sixteen-and-a-half minute video**,
which is most of my homework. And in those thirty seconds the map does essentially nothing:
Rhodesia appears, South Africa's colonies merge. The change-cards flick to a new set every
few seconds, each one truncated mid-sentence, so I read about a fifth of each. Meanwhile
the cards **overlap the year axis underneath them** — the numbers 1650, 1700, 1750 are
printed *behind* the cards. It looks broken, so I stop it.

**Minute 6.** I try clicking the year axis directly. It works — first click takes me to
1605. The map goes **almost completely empty**: Ireland, England, Jersey. Ten units. And the
caption underneath changes to **"Two engines are running at once — Atlantic and Company.
Two projects, two motors, one flag. The overlap is the point: nobody planned this."** That
is the first sentence in twenty minutes that made me think. Also — the four phase bars light
up in colour when you're inside them, which is the moment I understand what those bars are.

That understanding cost six minutes and was an accident.

**Minute 7.** I click the axis again to go forward. **Nothing happens.** I click again.
Nothing. The reason (measured): when the year changes, the number of change-cards changes,
so the whole timeline **re-flows vertically by ±21px** and the axis physically moves out from
under my cursor onto the "HOW FAST, AND HOW MUCH" strip, which swallows the click. The main
control of the app slides away from you while you use it.

**Minute 8.** I give up on the axis and use the −1 / +1 buttons. Which is a year at a time,
across four hundred years.

**Minute 9.** I press **the "Open the full key"** button because it's the only thing that
looks like a proper button. This opens a two-column panel that eats **44% of the screen**
and shoves the map off the right-hand edge — the Americas are now sliced off — while the
"ASK THESE THREE" card **floats on top of the map** obscuring the North Atlantic. The
content of the key is superb: fourteen legal forms, each with a one-line definition
("Protectorate: Not annexed, but Britain decides." "Princely state: An Indian ruler under
British paramountcy."), and a criticism column with real arithmetic — *"29 units lie 40° or
more from the equator and cover 11 million km²; 101 lie within the tropics and cover 13
million km² — 1.2 times as much ground, drawn smaller."* And it ends with an instruction:
**"Press 2 and 3 and watch how much of it goes."**

Fourteen categories is more than I can hold. But the instruction is clear, so I do it.

**Minute 11 — the best moment in the app.** I press **3**.

Canada goes. Australia goes. Most of Africa goes. **182 units → 94.** And a panel appears
saying **"THE WORD CHANGED, NOT THE MAP — Nothing was taken or given up in 1900. The word
'British' was redefined, and 88 units cross the line: 0 start counting, 88 stop."** Under it,
chips: *Ontario and 7 more (Canada) — Dominion. Jammu and Kashmir — Princely state. Egypt —
Military occupation.*

That is a genuinely great teaching moment. I got it. The pink map is a lie because "British"
is a word with a dial on it.

**And the screen is, at that exact moment, physically unreadable.** The definition panel,
the change-cards, the year axis (1200 / 1600 / 1650 / 1700 …), the rate rail and the "HOW
FAST" line are all **drawn on top of each other** in the same 200 pixels. Words overprint
words. `10 acts dated 1900 · 3 change the m…` has the numbers 1600 and 1650 printed through
it. Four separate layers of type in the same space. The best moment in the app happens
inside a rendering failure.

**Minute 12.** I press **4** (influenced). The map goes to 191 units and… I can barely tell.
Argentina and Afghanistan get faint grey outlines. The idea — *Britain ran places it never
claimed* — is the most surprising idea in the entire app and its visual expression is a hairline
I have to hunt for. The overlap wreck below is still there and now says
`"controlled" → "influenced"`.

**Minute 13.** I press **W** (Weight). India inflates into an enormous red mass, Canada and
Australia shrivel to specks. **This is spectacular and it lands instantly.** But there is no
sentence anywhere that says *"the map now sizes places by how many people lived in them."*
What it says instead, in grey, is: *"And what have you switched on? **SIZE** a cited
quantity, not land area — 176 carry a figure at 1900, 6 a hole."* I do not know what "6 a
hole" means. Also the byline box grew a fourth line, which pushed the legend down, which
**clipped "Open the full key" in half**.

**Minute 14.** I press **H** (Silences). Nothing happens on the map. The only feedback is
grey text: *"EMPTY SHAPES none at 1900; earliest destroyed 1910."* So the feature does
nothing at the year the app starts you on. I don't press it again.

**Minute 15.** I go back to the change-cards and click **"the rest of this account →"** on
Transvaal Colony. **Nothing visible happens.** The card gets a border. That's it.

I check this properly afterwards, and this is the thing I would put first in a bug report:
the drawer that opens contains **2,053 characters** of the best prose in the whole
application — *"took Pretoria in June 1900 and proclaimed the republic annexed on 1
September… Its independence, its gold revenue and, in the camps, a tenth of its Boer
population… African communities of the Transvaal: they were promised the vote by British
prop[aganda]…"* — and it is rendered at **y = 772 with a height of 1 pixel**, i.e. below the
bottom of a 768px window, in a container one pixel tall. On a 1920×1080 screen it gets all
of **59 pixels**, and prints its own epitaph in it: *"▼ 8 of 8 still below this line.
Nothing here is cut short — scroll, or use the arrow keys."* **The page cannot scroll.**
`scrollHeight` equals the viewport height. The app knows it is hiding something and tells you
to do a thing it has disabled.

The same 1px drawer swallows:
- **"account disputed — why?"** (the honesty affordance),
- **"Guess the widest year first →"** — which is *the only prediction question in the whole
  application*. I clicked it. Nothing happened. It builds a real question ("In which year
  was this map at its widest? Say a year before the atlas tells you") into a container
  **13 pixels tall holding 149 pixels of content**.

So the one moment the app asks *me* anything, at the viewport most school laptops have, is
invisible.

**Minute 17.** I try it on my phone out of curiosity. The map is **390 × 165 px** and a
solid opaque panel is parked **on top of two-thirds of it** — I can see the words "…ab",
"…residency", Fiji, Queensland. There is no year readout, no Play, no −1/+1, no change
cards, no timeline transport at all. The page is 844px and does not scroll. The phase bar's
fourth phase is labelled just **"IV"**. Phone is not an option.

**Minute 19.** I go back to the laptop and re-read the four phase bars, because they're the
only thing that looks like a story. I read the two captions I can reach. They are excellent.
That is genuinely all the connected narrative I found in twenty minutes.

**Minute 20.** I stop. Not because I'm annoyed — because I've run out of things I can find
that reward the effort of finding them.

## Part 3 — what I would tell a friend

> "It's a really detailed map of the British Empire where you can move a slider to change
> the year, and if you press number keys it changes what counts as 'British' and loads of
> countries disappear, which is actually kind of clever. But it's absolutely covered in
> stuff and half the buttons don't do anything when you click them."

Note what is not in that sentence: **anything that happened**. No story, no cause, no person.

## Part 4 — the test. What do I actually know?

Marked honestly: **[REAL]** = I could say this from the twenty minutes.
**[RECON]** = I only know it from reading the docs afterwards, not from using the app.

**Q1. When did the empire start?**
> *"Somewhere around 1600? There was nothing on the map at 1605 except Ireland and England
> and Jersey, and there's a bar that starts at about 1585."*

**[REAL, but weak and accidental.]** I got it from scrubbing to a nearly-blank map, not from
being told. I could not tell you *what happened* in 1585 or 1600 — no Roanoke, no Jamestown,
no charter. I saw the labels "c.1585–1838" and "1600–1858" on two bars and inferred.
I could not say what a "unit" is, and I would give the date with a shrug.

**Q2. When was it biggest?**
> *"1945? There's a red '+36 1945' on one of the graphs."*

**[REAL but for the wrong reason, and I think I'm wrong.]** +36 in 1945 is a *rate* — the
biggest one-year gain — not the peak. The app's own prediction question about this exists
and **is unreachable at my screen size**. So the app asked me the right question, hid it,
and left me to misread a different chart. This is the single clearest case of great
pedagogy defeated by layout.

**Q3. Name five things Britain took, and how.**
> Transvaal — taken by conquest, 1 Sept 1900, after invading over gold and miners' votes.
> Orange River Colony — conquest, 24 May 1900, because it honoured a treaty with the Transvaal.
> Tonga — protectorate declared, 18 May 1900, treaty signed by King George Tupou II.
> Niue — protectorate, 21 April 1900, after ignoring its kings' petitions for 21 years.
> Bengal — taken by conquest, 12 August 1765, from Shah Alam II and the Nawabs of Bengal.

**[REAL — and this is the app at its best.]** Five, with dates, mechanisms and named losers.
Better than I'd get from a textbook page. **But look at the sample:** four of the five are
1900, because that is the year the app parks you on and never leaves. I have Tonga and Niue
and no America, no Caribbean, no Australia. I have a random cross-section of one year, not
the shape of an empire. The dataset is clearly enormous; the app gave me one slice of it.

**Q4. Name three that fought their way out.**
> *"…India? Because there was a thing that said 'Partitioned, 15 August 1947'. And Ireland's
> on there somewhere."*

**[NOT REAL. I am failing this question.]** I met no independence movement, no named
anticolonial figure, no war of decolonisation. Not one. Nothing in the twenty minutes took
me past 1912 except one accidental click. There is no Gandhi, no Nkrumah, no Kenyatta, no
Kimathi in anything I saw. There is no sign anywhere on first paint that such people exist
in this app. Given LO6 requires six named colonised actors, I met **zero**.

**Q5. One sentence to explain the whole thing.**
> *"Britain took over loads of places at different times and then lost them, and the map
> makes it look simpler than it was."*

**[REAL, and it is thin.]** The second half is a real thing the app taught me — genuinely.
The first half is what I already believed when I opened the tab. The app has a much better
sentence than mine, several of them, and I found two of them: *"Two engines are running at
once — Atlantic and Company. Two projects, two motors, one flag. The overlap is the point:
nobody planned this."* and *"THE WORD CHANGED, NOT THE MAP."* Neither is anywhere near the
top of the visual hierarchy. Both are printed in the same size as a chart caption.

**Score, honestly: 2½ out of 5.** For content this good, that is a heartbreaking number, and
none of it is the content's fault.

## Part 5 — what is actually going on

Every one of the four pieces here is, on its own, better than the textbook chapter. Each one
also assumes it is the main event, and each one therefore opens with its full explanatory
apparatus visible. So at second zero there are **four separate "here is how to think about
this" panels** competing — the byline's three questions, the legend's how-to-read, the
definition switch's gloss, and the timeline's rate caption — and **not one sentence saying
what this is or what happened.**

The app has no first sentence. It has four fourth sentences.

And the second problem compounds it: **at 768px of height the app runs out of room and starts
dropping its own payloads into 1-pixel containers.** Four of its promised interactions
(*rest of this account*, *account disputed*, *guess the widest year*, the whole timeline
drawer) are dead there, and the definition-change panel overprints the axis. The app was
composed for ~1080px of height and degrades not gracefully but catastrophically at the
height most of its audience actually has.

Nothing needs to be deleted. It needs an order.
