## Existing strengths to preserve

- The guide derives student prompts and rubrics from `rallye-content.js`, reducing the risk of a separate answer key drifting from the live task.
- Model responses are examples, not compulsory conclusions. Alternative interpretations and misconceptions are supplied for every stop.
- It separates official claim from implementation and public opinion; distinguishes legal status from belonging; avoids treating independence as the end of every relationship.
- It names annual map snapshots, contemporary geographic approximations, limited connectivity, open teacher answers and the need for human marking.
- It avoids demanding a student disclose ancestry or speak on behalf of an identity group.

## Proposed teacher-guide structure

1. **Purpose and Q2 English competence focus.** One paragraph establishes reading → analysis → written comment, with empire as contextual evidence for plural British identities.
2. **Place in the unit.** Name the confirmed primary set and the lesson bridge. Retain clearly labelled optional class-copy links.
3. **60-minute lesson integration.** Give the explicit 5 + 45 + 7 + 3-minute structure; within the rallye use only the task author's final accepted timing. The 45-minute estimate assumes a prepared, short evidence pack, established navigation and the indicated passages rather than unrestricted web browsing.
4. **Source use.** Distinguish original excerpt, institutional contextual account, editorial summary and map. Classroom readings remain in teacher-issued copies; no full copyrighted text is shipped.
5. **Station and final keys.** Exact prompt, operator/product, current word guidance, source requirement, model reasoning, acceptable alternatives, misconceptions and matching rubric.
6. **Formative feedback.** Give one content/evidence next step and one English-language next step. English-language performance is relevant in an English course; vocabulary support should not erase that aim. Do not infer marks from word count or automated completion.
7. **Background and source directory.** Preserve the substantive historical notes and source links while keeping their role distinct from curriculum authority.

## Third primary document: material mapping

`UK_Q2_Lehrwerksrecherche_SH_2026_27.pdf` distinguishes its evidence/access levels on PDF p. 5; its material list is not proof that every licensed reading has been inspected. Teacher references therefore require checking edition, licence, excerpt length, page number and media access.

- PDF p. 7: Pathway lists Hawkins's ironic guidebook, Tombs's newspaper argument and Hirsch's non-fiction extract. The app provides historical evidence to take into these genres, rather than replacing their readings.
- PDF p. 9: Green Line distinguishes its Windrush listening from Levy's essay and its comment/editorial skill work. A written app card is not listening practice.
- PDF p. 11: Context lists Taking Stock as a newspaper article, colonial heritage as video/art, Sanghera as non-fiction, and an argumentative writing lab.
- PDF p. 14: Abi-Box distinguishes visual/statistical changes in representation, a curriculum role-play and listening texts. These are optional next lessons with distinct competence aims.

The guide cites these source-file pages and describes its adjacency as a teaching adaptation, never as a claim to have reproduced the publisher's lesson or secured access to its material.
