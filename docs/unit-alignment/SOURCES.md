## Finding and bounded recommendation

The previous fifteen cards supplied useful historical orientation, provenance, limitations and explicitly labelled paraphrases. Only three included original words, with one of those optional. An offline learner could discuss evidence and purpose but could not analyse original diction at every station. The revised set distinguishes those kinds of evidence and supplies original wording for the two required analysis moves.

The historical enquiry and link to present British identities remain central. Existing Cotton and Victoria extracts are retained; verified wording was added to the existing Windrush and Kenya cards. Victoria and Hague support the two required wording-analysis moves, while the Windrush witness enriches the citizenship/belonging explanation without creating a third required language analysis. No new card was added; Hong Kong and Canada became optional context. Both final reference cards remain optional because the final argument reuses existing evidence. The map supports a separate media observation about selection, colour and omission; it cannot supply a participant's voice.

## Classroom-copy boundary

The classroom-text reviewer identified Fletcher's “The ideal of Britishness” in `British Identities Today.docx` and Hawkins's `The_Bloody_British_Paul_Hawkins_Extract.docx` as supplementary class readings. Their satire and metaphors can bridge representation, national branding and selective memory to the historical evidence. Reference teacher-provided copies when a lesson actually uses them; do not ship whole readings or make the core offline route depend on them. A satirical national portrait is evidence of a representation and its possible reader effect, not a survey of British people. The vocabulary sheet names a Mattinson article but does not supply that article's complete text; do not manufacture its claims.
