### Student paraphrase (85 words, our own language)

In 2022, British Museum collection manager Imran Javed described a display combining museum objects with his own possessions. One was a bracelet bearing the name of his family's village in Punjab. His Punjabi Muslim ancestors had left that village during Partition and settled in what is now Pakistan. Javed commissioned the bracelet to remember their journey and a home they had lost. By including it in a London museum display, he brought his family history into a public account of South Asian heritage in Britain.

**Scope:** One person's interpretation and curatorial choices; neither a survey of British South Asians nor evidence of how visitors responded. This family must remain distinct from Iqbal's aunt in the earlier archive source. Do not imply that all South Asian migration to Britain resulted from Partition.

### Alternative local paraphrase (75 words)

In 2017, the Memories of Independence project invited Indian and Pakistani families in Glasgow to share photographs and stories about Partition, independence and moving to Scotland. Volunteers recorded oral histories and learned to build a public online photographic archive. The project also explored how younger generations viewed their families' moves. This shows a community choosing to preserve and share this history in present-day Britain. The project description does not tell us what every participant thought.
