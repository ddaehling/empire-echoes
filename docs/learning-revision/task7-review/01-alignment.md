# Independent review 1 — constructive alignment

Date: 2026-10-01. Pre-revision build: `enquiry-analysis-2026-10-01`.

Reviewed the actual student content in `app/journey/js/rallye-content.js` and `rallye-resources.js`, its rendering rules in `rallye.js`, the product brief, and the matched teacher content. This is an independent curriculum review, not a report of a classroom trial.

## Verdict: revision required

A well-prepared learner can answer task 7 using Windrush and Kenya. The sequence does **not yet reliably prepare an independent Q2/B2 learner to choose two cases freely from the six stops and explain British identities today**. Four stops develop useful historical or analytical skills without supplying the contemporary evidence needed for the final inference. The final task adds the most important reasoning demand—explaining a documented connection across time—after most station responses have finished.

The weakness is alignment, not lack of difficulty. The current tasks now require comparison, interpretation and qualification. Adding more historical recall questions would not solve it.

## What the final task actually demands

Task 7 (`rallye-content.js`, finalAssessment, lines 365–413) requires students to:

1. Establish a particular imperial relationship or event.
2. Use evidence about a later/present form of belonging, public memory or an idea about Britain.
3. Explain the connection between the two, rather than merely list an old fact and a modern opinion.
4. Weigh how much that connection explains, maintaining the difference between individuals, institutions and populations.
5. Bring two cases together into an accessible school-magazine argument.

The definition of plural identities in the introduction is helpful. The final prompt also reasonably permits a specific evidential limitation instead of requiring researched knowledge of a competing cause. But the current two sentence stems supply connective wording, not practice in constructing or weighing the historical-to-present connection.

## Teacher content cannot fill the student gap

The teacher background contains good explanations of migration, public memory and the limits of state evidence. The final model answer uses Jamaica and Windrush and discusses remembering whose actions mattered. That demonstrates a reasonable *interpretation* of a possible public account; it does not add an observed contemporary Jamaica/Britain memory example to the core student evidence. Independent success must not depend on finding and copying the teacher model or hearing an unplanned teacher explanation.

The teacher extension “Trace one named institution, place or object in Britain back to an imperial connection” describes precisely the missing kind of explanatory work. It currently occurs after or outside the core route.

## Minimal remedy

Keep the seven-stop structure and existing analytical work. Do not add more stops simply to raise difficulty.

- **Make the connection visible before the final comment.** On suitable earlier stops, pair the historical material with a short, verifiable modern evidence card. The task should require explaining how the earlier history helps interpret that modern evidence. A single integrated question can replace, rather than append to, the existing task.
- **Give genuine choice.** Windrush and Kenya already work. Add at least two different, supported routes, for example a real British museum/public-memory intervention about slavery and a British act of remembering partition with a clearly identified participant or institution. If all six stops remain presented as equally usable final cases, each needs a documented later connection; otherwise visibly distinguish foundational historical stops from the case choices that have contemporary evidence. An unqualified “choose any two” must not lead students to unsupported inference.
- **Retain the historical reasoning.** Company power and Crown-rule rhetoric can remain valuable foundation work. Do not manufacture a modern causal claim merely so every stop has the same format. If paired with later evidence, ask what the comparison supports and what it cannot establish.
- **Require one rehearsal of the extent judgement.** Kenya already rehearses a qualified judgement about recognition. Extend that into the final problem: what aspect of Britain's public self-presentation is explained here, and why does that not establish what everyone in Britain thinks? In Windrush, distinguish an imperial connection from later policy choices rather than treating empire as an all-purpose cause.
- **Make task 7 a synthesis.** Provide a short, visible planning instruction: for each case identify the earlier relationship, a later piece of evidence, the link you can explain, and the limit of that explanation; then decide what the two cases together justify. This is scaffolding for reasoning, not an answer template or grading rubric. Keep the student's choice and judgement open.
- **Respect dated evidence.** Name the dates of 2013 and 2020 evidence and frame them as modern examples bearing on the question. Do not silently turn them into evidence of the current views of the whole population.

## Acceptance conditions for independent re-review

1. Without opening external sites or teacher notes, a learner can choose two supported cases and explain a specific historical-to-modern connection in each.
2. Every offered final-case route has accessible modern evidence, or its narrower foundational role is explicit. At least four materially different choices should be available if the interface continues to suggest broad selection across the journey.
3. At least two earlier assignments make students perform the historical-to-modern reasoning, so task 7 combines practised moves instead of introducing them.
4. The relevant modern evidence is essential and rendered in the assignment; it is neither a hidden optional link nor a teacher-only explanation.
5. The prepared material distinguishes an individual feeling, an institutional statement, a public-memory intervention and a population-wide claim. Causal language matches that evidential scope.
6. Students have practised or are explicitly prompted to weigh explanatory strength, not merely append “not everyone” after two descriptive paragraphs.
7. The final prompt permits disagreement and partial explanations. It does not reward a predetermined pro- or anti-empire conclusion or imply that empire explains every aspect of identity.
8. Prompts remain appropriately demanding for Q2/B2 and keep one coherent response per stop. Added source reading is reflected in honest timing guidance; no grades, response-length requirements or recall gates are introduced.

### Verdict: approve the revised student sequence, with the limits below

The substantive alignment failure is repaired. Every freely selectable case now supplies a defensible historical-to-modern connection through essential, in-app evidence. Students practise this connection before task 7, and the final task explicitly asks them to compare explanatory strength and make their limitation affect their judgement. It no longer depends on inferring present identities from historical facts alone.

All fifteen pairs of the six cases are now usable for a bounded final argument. Some comparisons are deliberately narrower: Company/Jamaica mainly illuminate competing public-memory choices; Monarchy/Kenya mainly illuminate official self-presentation. That is acceptable because the final task explicitly permits public memory and ideas about Britain as well as personal belonging. The material gives students grounds to identify this narrower explanatory reach instead of pretending every pair explains all British identities.

### Why this is preparation rather than answer provision

The modern cards give students something observable to explain, while the prompts leave the consequential judgement open. For example, the Clive task does not tell students whether a retained statue is right; the Jamaica task requires a justified editorial choice; and the royal/settlement cases allow students to interpret both change and continuity. The supplied source limits prevent unsupported generalisation but do not settle how much importance to give the connection.

The revised Windrush case is particularly useful for the “how far” demand: its 2022 policy explanation lets students distinguish an imperial relationship from later decisions. This is stronger than merely adding “not everyone” to a conclusion. The final discussion prompt also correctly distinguishes a weak explanation from evidence that cannot establish a wider claim.

The final-stage ONS card is now essential and correctly limited to England and Wales in 2021. It can discipline an overbroad claim about identity; it cannot quantify imperial influence. The final task says this explicitly. It does not require unsupported speculation about class, religion or another competing cause: a relevant evidential limit remains a legitimate route.

In `rallye.js`, `essentialSources()` and `evidenceGroups()` place the new material in the core evidence rather than optional external research. The final `earlierResponses()` brings all six cases' responses, notes and essential evidence into named disclosures. These disclosures function as the requested case-selection interface; students are explicitly directed to open their chosen cases. They do not hide missing prerequisite research. The prompts retain one response per stop and introduce no grades or length gates.

### Limits and handoff notes

- This is expert-agent content review, not observed student performance, accessibility testing, or independent verification of every underlying web source. Approval is for the exact student-content candidate above; the teacher revision was still in progress when reviewed.
- The seven-stop estimate is now 60 minutes: 40 minutes across six preparatory responses and 20 for the final comment. The reading and inference demands are substantial, especially in the proclamation and three-source Windrush cases. Treat 60 minutes as an ambitious, flexible planning estimate for prepared B2 learners; allow a second session or additional time. Do not state that an unfamiliar class can reliably finish the sequence in one hour without a classroom trial. This timing caution does not require weakening the tasks.
- The modern evidence dates from 2013–2024, with ONS data from 2021. It supports dated examples relevant to the present, not claims about every institution's or person's position in 2026. This distinction is already made in the student content and should remain in teacher guidance.
- The route now prepares a reasoned answer to the stated task. It does not become a comprehensive account of British identities, and a well-supported answer may conclude that these sources explain particular public narratives more directly than individual self-identification.

No blocking change to the reviewed student content is requested.
