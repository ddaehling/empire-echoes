# Task 7 source research: monarchy, Commonwealth and colonial memory

Checked 1 October 2026. These are dated examples of institutional self-presentation, not evidence that all people in Britain share an identity or opinion. All proposed student links are freely readable HTML. No app files edited.

## Recommended task 3 connection

Use three moments: Victoria's 1858 proclamation, the change in India's constitutional relationship recorded in 1949, and Charles III's 2024 Commonwealth address. The mechanism is the monarchy's changed institutional relationship with formerly ruled countries, not merely similar royal vocabulary. A reader can investigate how a British institution presents international links inherited from empire while recognising that independent states have agency. The proclamation alone cannot establish such a present-day connection.

### Historical bridge: the London Declaration

- Primary text: [London Declaration, 1949](https://thecommonwealth.org/london-declaration-1949), Commonwealth Secretariat HTML transcription; issued at the London meeting of 22–27 April 1949.
- Supports: India's decision to become a republic and remain a Commonwealth member; acceptance of the King as symbolic head of an association of independent countries. This differs from Crown government in India. Avoid implying that today's King governs India or that the Commonwealth is simply the empire renamed.
- Suggested context: **India became independent in 1947. In 1949, Commonwealth governments agreed that India could remain a member when it became a republic. The King would symbolise the association of independent countries, rather than rule India.**
- Additional institutional history: [Our history](https://thecommonwealth.org/history). It states that members choose the Head of the Commonwealth; the British monarch does not automatically hold the position. Some members were never British colonies.

## Recommended task 6 connection

The 2013 settlement has a direct later history: a memorial opened in 2015; the King discussed colonial violence in Kenya in 2023; Kenyan campaigners still called for fuller acknowledgement. This supplies dated evidence that public memory and Britain's official self-presentation remained contested after settlement. It does not establish the distribution of opinion within Britain or prove a single unchanged policy across governments.

## Verified alternative: a physical memorial

For a shorter task, the 2015 memorial can replace the two 2023 texts. [UK–Kenya shared history: Recognition and Reconciliation](https://www.gov.uk/government/speeches/uk-kenya-shared-history-recognition-and-reconciliation) is the full text of British High Commissioner Christian Turner's speech, delivered 12 September 2015 in Nairobi. It explicitly ties the memorial's opening to the promise in Hague's 2013 settlement and identifies the steering committee's Kenyan and British participants.

Original proposed student paraphrase (78 words): **In September 2015, British High Commissioner Christian Turner spoke at the opening of a memorial in Nairobi for people tortured or ill-treated during colonial rule. He connected it directly with the commitment announced in the 2013 settlement. British officials, Kenyan veterans, a human rights organisation and Kenyan public bodies had worked together on the project. Turner presented the memorial as an acknowledgement of suffering and a contribution to reconciliation, while also wanting Britain and Kenya to move forward.**

Limit: this documents an act of commemoration and Turner's interpretation in 2015; it cannot establish how visitors interpret the monument today or whether reconciliation was achieved. The 2023 records offer a more recent, explicitly unsettled comparison.

## Primary-source verification trail

- [Hague's statement, 6 June 2013](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/statement-to-parliament-on-settlement-of-mau-mau-claims): settlement concerning 5,228 claimants; £19.9 million including costs; planned Nairobi memorial; denial of liability. It calls recognition of error and future cooperation features of British democracy. That last point supplies an existing explicit link to official ideas about Britain.
