# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Secondary-school history students, around age 16, and their teachers. Students work on school laptops, tablets and phones; teachers introduce and discuss the map on a classroom projector. The existing DIDACTIC_SPEC.md establishes this audience and the historical learning objectives.

## Product Purpose

Help a class understand how the British Empire expanded, how different kinds of power worked, and how people challenged colonial rule. A student should have an obvious first action, a manageable lesson, and a way to check understanding. The full territorial dataset is reference material, not a memorisation task.

## Brand Personality

Clear, thoughtful, inviting. Preserve the historical atlas identity, careful typography and evidence-led approach described in docs/DESIGN.md, while responding to the user's explicit request for a much simpler classroom experience.

## Anti-references

The existing screen's dense controls, tiny annotations, competing instructional prompts, and overwhelming legal-status legend. Avoid vintage pastiche, decorative dashboard statistics, endless panels and scholarly jargon in student instructions.

## Design Principles

1. Give each screen one clear classroom job: explore, learn, or recall.
2. Let the map explain change; reveal detail when the student asks for it.
3. Teach with short questions and named people, not encyclopaedic walls of text.
4. Keep sources, uncertainty and the limitations of historical maps discoverable.
5. Make lessons usable on a projector and on an individual student's device.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Maintain keyboard access, visible focus, readable contrast, non-colour status cues, reduced-motion support, and responsive layouts. Target WCAG AA. No account or internet dependency for the classroom experience. Historical violence and coercion should be named plainly and without sensational imagery.
